Truck Finance Options Australia Explained

If you run a trade, a freight run or a delivery route, the truck is often the single biggest asset purchase the business will make. This guide looks at truck finance options buyers typically weigh up, and how those options sit inside the wider asset and equipment finance market described on The Loan Phone finance hub.

The parent page frames its service around one idea: a personal asset and equipment finance expert works with more than 100 lenders to source a solution for a borrower's situation. It positions the offer as business finance for busy Australians, lists truck finance as one of five solution areas alongside car finance, equipment finance, asset finance and business finance, and presents a simple three step flow: tell the team what you need, the expert compares options, then you discuss the solution at a preferred time.

The finance paths available for a working truck

On the parent site, the inquiry form asks two questions before anything else: what are you financing, and what do you need to do with it. The financing menu covers a car or commercial vehicle, an asset or equipment, business finance, or something else. The purpose menu is purchase, refinance, or other. Those two menus shape the realistic paths:

  • Truck finance: listed as a dedicated solution on the parent page, suited when the truck itself is the asset being funded.
  • Car or commercial vehicle finance: where the truck is handled as part of a broader vehicle purchase.
  • Equipment finance: where the truck is one item within a wider equipment package.
  • Asset finance: a broader label that covers trucks and other business assets under one umbrella.
  • Business finance: for owners funding more than the vehicle alone.

The amount brackets on the form run from under $25,000 through to $25,000 to $100,000, $100,000 to $250,000, and $250,000 and above, with a not sure yet option for borrowers still working out the figure. That range matters because a single rigid product rarely fits both a first owner driver and an expanding fleet.

Who this applies to

Truck finance, as described on the parent page, is business finance for busy Australians. It applies most directly to:

  • Owner drivers buying a first truck or upgrading an existing one.
  • Trades and small operators adding a commercial vehicle to the business.
  • ABN holders, with ABN holder loans listed among the site's services.
  • Existing borrowers who want to refinance a vehicle already on the books.

The refinance choice on the inquiry form is worth noting. It shows the process is not only for new purchases; an owner whose current arrangement no longer fits the business can use the same form to start a refinance conversation.

How the comparison process works

The parent page outlines a three step flow. First, you tell the team what you need: the finance type, an approximate amount bracket, and whether the purpose is purchase or refinance. Second, an asset and equipment finance expert compares suitable lender options, drawing on a panel described as more than 100 lenders. Third, you talk through the suitable options and next steps at a preferred callback time.

Two contact routes are offered. You can call the direct phone line and speak with a loan expert with no obligation, or you can submit a callback request with your name, phone number, an optional email address and a preferred time. The callback form offers morning, afternoon, evening and anytime slots, and the page describes the first step as quick and obligation free, with consent collected through tick boxes before your details are used.

Things to check before you commit

Before signing any truck loan, read the details in the credit proposal and contract rather than relying on a summary. The parent page states clearly that information it provides is not financial or credit advice, so borrowers should read the documents and, where needed, seek independent guidance before proceeding.

In Australia, credit providers and credit brokers must hold an Australian credit licence from the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, and you can verify a licence through ASIC's registers before you commit. Practical checks worth making include the total amount repayable over the life of the loan, any fees, whether the rate is fixed or variable, and what happens if you want to pay the loan out early.

Common questions

What amounts can I apply for? The inquiry form on the parent page offers four brackets: under $25,000, $25,000 to $100,000, $100,000 to $250,000, and $250,000 and above, plus a not sure yet option if you have not settled on a figure.

Is requesting finance options obligation free? The page describes the first step as quick and obligation free. Before your details are used, you tick boxes confirming consent to be contacted and agreement with the privacy policy.

Can I request a callback instead of calling? Yes. The page offers both a direct phone line and a callback request form. The form lets you nominate a preferred time: morning, afternoon, evening or anytime.
